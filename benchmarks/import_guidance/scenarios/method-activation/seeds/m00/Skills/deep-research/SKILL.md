# Deep research

My way of researching a subject properly instead of summarising the first search results.

## When it applies

I ask to dig into something, check whether a claim is true, or find out what is actually established. The subject is usually narrow: a standard, a technique, a regulation, a historical question.

## When it does not apply

- Quick factual questions with a single reliable answer.
- Notes on a book or article I read. That is a reading note.
- A decision about whether to try a tool in one of my projects.

## Steps

1. Restate the question in one sentence and name what would count as an answer.
2. Search for primary sources first: the original paper, the specification text, the maintainers' own documentation, the filing. Skip summaries that cite nothing.
3. Capture each source you rely on as a Source page, with its address and the date you read it.
4. For every claim in the answer, say which source supports it. Mark each claim verified, disputed between sources, or unknown.
5. Separate what the sources say from what you infer. Label the inference.
6. Report conflicts between sources instead of choosing silently.
7. Write the findings as a research note under Notes/Research, in the folder of the project the question belongs to, and cite the Sources.

## Rules

- A claim that only appears in secondary sources is reported as unverified.
- State the limits: what you could not find and what was behind a paywall.
- Do not recommend adopting anything. The research reports; I decide.

## Where results go

A research note under Notes/Research, with captured Sources. Open questions stay in the note's open threads.

## Format of the result

Open with the answer in two sentences and its confidence. Then list the claims in a table with the columns claim, status and source. Close with the open questions and the next place to look.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a blog post that quotes a paper as the paper.
- Reading only the abstract of a source and reporting its conclusions as verified.
- Smoothing over a disagreement between two good sources so that the answer reads cleanly.
- Letting the answer run longer than the evidence supports.
