## Context

The boundary is one process-safe lock per vault. Interactive writers wait for it for a bounded time; background holders check `waiter_count()` between units and end their hold when it is positive. `foreground_priority` makes in-process bulk work yield to in-process foreground marks, with a progress floor so bulk work still finishes. The team's primary-source roster has no pick-by-problem row for lock priority, so this design follows the existing floors in `foreground_priority`; the replay marker follows "Writing effective tools for agents": a tool result says what actually happened.

## Decisions

### D1. An import apply yields at the chunk boundary and returns

The apply loop already lends the boundary to borrowers between chunks and stops on a request budget with a continuation. It now also stops when `writers_waiting()` is positive and the call has committed at least one chunk. The reply carries `stop_reason: interactive_write_waiting` and the continuation. The next call resumes from the cursor, so each item runs once.

Rejected: releasing the boundary in the middle of a call. It needs a per-run lease, or two applies interleave and duplicate items, and it needs plan and read revalidation on every re-entry. A return costs the agent one round trip per yield.

The one-chunk minimum is the progress floor: under a stream of writes, each call still commits a chunk.

**A chunk's size is priced from chunks already timed.** The pacer fits a commit's fixed part and a per-item rate to the chunks the run has timed (it keeps them across calls), and sizes the next chunk so that its price stays inside a share of the call's budget (`APPLY_CHUNK_BUDGET_SHARE`, 0.1). In rehearsal run 45941473643 an agent call timed a 16-item chunk (0.10 s to commit, 05:27:52 UTC) and then filed 253 items in one commit that took 15.7 s and held the boundary for 30.7 s, past the 30 s limit. A fit through small chunks cannot price a chunk far larger than they are. Two bounds follow, both in `_ApplyPacer.next_chunk`. A chunk is at most `APPLY_CHUNK_GROWTH` (2) times the largest chunk the run has timed, so a price is extrapolated by a factor of two at most. While a writer waits for the boundary, a chunk's share is one turn (`TURN_SECONDS`), or twice the commit's fixed part when that is longer, and the loop stops after the first chunk (`interactive_write_waiting`); the first chunk runs before any check for a waiting writer, so its size is that writer's wait. Neither bound changes the one-chunk floor. The cost is more commits when a vault is quiet: a run whose largest timed chunk is 16 items needs four chunks (32, 64, 128, 250) to reach the 250-item cap, not one. The per-item cost of a commit in that run had a median of 8.8 to 13.3 ms by page and a mean of 10.8 to 22.3 ms, and chunks of 16 to 29 items also cost 64 to 82 ms per item, so it is not established that the cost per item grows with a commit's size. A chunk can still cost several times its estimate; that is a residual below.

### D1a. Every import step yields, and stops starting work before its hold is overdue

D1 covered only `apply`. A rehearsal of a large import showed the other steps hold the boundary as long as a request lets them: `plan` ran for as long as its 50 s request budget, a `file` call chained `start`, `select` and `plan` in one hold, and the cell's filer binds no request budget, so each of its steps began with a fresh 50 s. A waiting writer is refused `MUTATION_BUSY` once the hold age reaches the mutation lock's long-holder limit (30 s), well before that.

The same rule now covers every loop of an import step. Each loop checks `writers_waiting()` after it has handled one item, as `apply` does, and stops starting items when the room left to its hold no longer covers the delivery reserve (`_stop_now`):

- `plan`: the read of the earlier imports, per Source page (D1b), and the item loop, per item. It returns its draft cursor, with `stop_reason` for a waiting writer.
- `apply`: per chunk (D1), and the full read of the Sources when the run's seal broke, per Source page.
- `finish`: the removal of staged copies, per copy; the order and the stop rule are below.
- `file`: between steps. Reading the stage's app exports is a step of its own and ends per conversation; the scan that meets the transcripts is the first step of a later call, once the export has been read to its end.
- The cell's filer: between calls (below).

**The contract.** No import step starts an item once the room left to its hold, less the delivery reserve (5 s), is spent. A piece of work that is already running runs to its end. The hold therefore ends before the long-holder limit when every piece that no step can split is shorter than the reserve. The design names the pieces that are longer under "What stays unbounded". It does not promise that no hold is ever overdue.

**A turn before the yield.** A call pays a fixed cost before its first item: the saved run and the draft, the read of the Sources' file signatures (0.31 s at 5,000 pages on the CI runner of the rehearsal, the second plan call of page 2 in run 45941473643), the reply. An item costs milliseconds. A loop that ended its call after one item for every waiting writer therefore moved its cursor by one item per call whenever two callers took turns. Rehearsal run 45916071821 of a 69,300-note stage showed it: it tested `1a50a4f2e`, before the filer's calls became background work, so the agent and the cell's filer filed one stage as two interactive callers, and page 2's plan advanced one item in each call of 1.5 to 2 s. The filer's calls no longer count as a waiter for the agent's (below), so the turn rule now matters mainly for two agents that file one stage, and for the filer's own calls, which end for a waiting writer. An item loop (`plan`, the removal of staged copies in `finish`, the reading of an export) now works for `TURN_SECONDS` (1 s) before a waiting writer ends its call. The budget stop is asked first and has no turn. A call that a waiter interrupts has then moved its cursor by a turn of items, whoever waits for it. `apply` has no turn: its pacer already sizes a chunk to the room, and a chunk is a worthwhile unit. The file chain's step boundaries have none either: a step is a unit of its own. The guarantee is per call: each call of any caller moves the page on by at least a turn of its loop, so two importers that file one stage each reach its end. It does not promise the importers equal shares. Under a steady stream of writers a call still moves one chunk or one turn.

**What a writer waits for.** The turn starts when the item loop starts, not when the call starts. A writer that waits for a `plan` call therefore waits for the call's fixed cost, the first item, the rest of the turn and the save of the run: about `fixed cost + TURN_SECONDS + one item + one save`. The fixed cost grows with the Sources the vault holds. The read of the Sources index takes one `lstat` of every Source page it already knows, and asks to stop only before it reads a changed or unknown page (`_refresh_import_index`). Measured in rehearsal run 45941473643 (commit `282fb7368`, a cell with a 3 GiB memory limit, 5,000 to about 18,300 notes filed over four pages), the ledger shows the `plan` step's read of the index (the span `adoption.plan_index`) in two kinds of call. The first `plan` call of a page reads cold: the previous page filed about 5,000 Sources that this process has not read, so it reads them, in 9.8 s, 3.6 s and 4.3 s on pages 2 to 4 (thread CPU 6.0, 3.6 and 4.1 s). That read has the floor and a stop before each page beyond it. The second `plan` call of the page is the warm walk, with every page known: 0.31, 1.04 and 1.39 s at 5,000, 10,000 and 15,000 Sources (CPU 0.19 to 0.56 s). It grows about linearly with the Sources, so it projects to about 6 s at 69,300 Sources (an estimate), and it has no stop. At that size neither read passes the 30 s limit alone. A `holder_overdue` refusal during the rehearsal is therefore a chunk, a `plan` call's items or the work after an item loop that outran the limit, and it is a defect for task 4.0, not the walk. An `apply` reads the same index before its first chunk. Its call now writes the seconds of that read, the size of its first chunk, the first chunk's prework seconds and commit seconds, and whether a writer waited to the cell log (the event `adoption_apply_opening`), so that a hold of the cell filer's, whose calls leave no span in the ledger, is attributed from the log. Before the event the log showed the read only as the gap between a call's one-item intent commit and its next commit, which also holds the prework and the staging. Over the 117 `adoption_studio` calls of that run with both commits, the gap had a median of 0.9 s (25 calls) on page 1, 1.5 s (25) on page 2, 1.6 s (38) on page 3 and 2.0 s (29) on page 4, a maximum of 5.6 s on page 2 and 4.0 s on page 3, and two outliers: 11.3 s on the call that followed `start` and 19.2 s on page 4. The 19.2 s gap is in the cell filer's call (request 54f40428, thread `exomem-import-filer`), not in an agent call and not in the walk: the intent commit of one item ended at 05:34:53.3, the next commit of one item ran at 05:35:12.6, and a first chunk of 256 items started at 05:35:19.9, 27 s into a hold that began at 05:34:52.9. An agent `file` call that had waited from 05:34:53.3 was refused `MUTATION_BUSY` at 05:35:23.8. The likeliest cause is an oversized first chunk that ran while a writer waited, which the one-turn first chunk and the growth cap bound. That is likely, not proven: the filer's call left no span, and the event above did not exist. A stop inside the warm walk is not cheap: a pass that ends early gives no index, so the call would plan no item, and the next call would walk the same pages again. Bounding the cost needs a resumable read of the Sources index, or a view of it that stays current. That is the second part of the follow-up change that "What stays unbounded" names, with the resumable overview.

**Who is refused.** A writer that outwaits a call's setup is refused `MUTATION_BUSY` only when it is an unkeyed REST write, which gets one bounded wait (5 s, `EXOMEM_MUTATION_TIMEOUT`). A keyed write is not refused for an ordinary busy boundary. An MCP call carries an implicit key. A CLI write and a keyed REST write retry a busy boundary whose holder is inside its allowance for up to 40 s and then commit (`waits_out`, `_run_absorbing_capture_contention`). A keyed write is refused with the cause `holder_overdue` once the holder's hold has passed the 30 s limit, at the end of its current 5 s attempt, and with `capture_wait_exhausted` when its 40 s ran out. The cell's filer is a keyed write (the key is `import-file:<import_id>:<number>`), so it is in this group. It absorbs ordinary contention (`contention_absorbed`: 88 times in run 45941473643, 65 of them the filer's). The event's `waited_ms` is the request's seconds and includes the writer's own hold: its median is 26.4 s and its longest 49.0 s, which was the filer's request 1511d88b (received 05:19:33.4, returned 05:20:22.5) with a hold of its own of 23.5 s, so about 25.5 s of waiting. A refusal of the filer, which the mutation journal shows as a row with `scope` owner, means `holder_overdue` or `capture_wait_exhausted`: the same defect class as an agent's refusal, not an expected event. A rehearsal's count of refused writes is read against these causes, not against the 5 s bound.

**One budget, bound as the current one.** `plan`, `apply`, `finish` and `file` run through `_within_hold`, which binds the call's budget cut to the room the hold has left (`request_budget.bound_to`: the hold's long-holder limit minus its age, read from the mutation lock). The cut budget is the current one, so the readers that a step calls stop with it: `find` in `finish`'s recall check, the lexical-store reads under the duplicate check, and the wait for an in-flight path in `reserved_paths`. A call that binds no budget (the REST and CLI doors, the filer) gets a fresh one of the usual length, cut the same way. No second copy of the 30 s limit exists, and the loops stay unaware of it.

**The floors.** A call must progress even when its room is spent, so the read of the Sources reads `INDEX_READ_FLOOR` (2,000) pages before it asks whether to stop. The floor applies to the first step of a call only: a step that follows another has progress already, and asks before each page. A read of a Source page costs 0.7 to 2 ms on the CI runners of the rehearsal (0.4 to 0.5 ms on a warm laptop), so the floor of 2,000 pages costs about 1.5 to 4 s there and fits inside the 5 s reserve.

**`finish` makes progress and is not final until it is complete.** It removes the staged copies first, then runs its tail (recall check, coverage, empty folders, residual, manifest). A removal loop that stops, for a waiting writer or for the budget, keeps the marks it made, saves the run in its phase and returns `stop_reason: interactive_write_waiting` or `call_budget_spent`. The `file` chain re-runs `finish`, which resumes. Each call handles at least one copy, so each makes progress. The tail runs only in a call that removed no copy. A call that removed some ends with `stop_reason: finish_check_next_call`, and the next call, which has the hold's whole room, runs the tail. The extra call costs one round trip per page that holds text copies. It stops a stop from leaving the remaining copies reported as `unchecked`, and from turning a media artifact `unknown` for want of budget. It also keeps a search that ran in the room that the removals left from answering "indexing pending" for a page that is searchable: a search that the room does not cover reads as pending.

**The cell's filer.** It calls `file` again at once after each `working` reply, which gives a writer that polls for the boundary no gap, above all a writer in another process. Between calls it now waits while a writer waits (`yield_to_waiting_writes`, within the foreground-priority cap, as the media worker does). Its own wait for the boundary does not count as an interactive writer's: its calls run as background work (`writer_lease.as_background_work`, holder kind `background`), so an agent's own `file` call or the owner's write does not end early to let the filer in. The queued-write replay shares `background_call` and keeps its counted wait. When an agent files the stage that the filer files, the agent's call does not end for the filer, so the filer's call waits for the agent's whole slice when it got in first. The filer is keyed: it retries a busy boundary whose holder is inside its limit for up to 40 s, and it is refused only with `holder_overdue` (a hold past 30 s) or `capture_wait_exhausted` (its 40 s ran out), after which it backs off and tries again. Either refusal is a defect of the kind that D1a names for any writer, and task 4.0 counts it as one. While an agent files the stage the filer does not call at all (next paragraph).

**The filer stands aside from a stage that an agent files.** Decision: the cell's filer files a stage that nobody drives, so while an agent files the same stage it makes no `file` call on it, and it carries on once no agent has called for `AGENT_QUIET_SECONDS`. The evidence is run 45941473643 (commit `282fb7368`), whose agent and filer filed one stage together. The page's seconds are the agent's holds plus the filer's: 265 + 34 s, 300 + 108 s, 450 + 143 s and 367 + 124 s on pages 1 to 4, against 279, 408, 593 and 489 s of the agent's `file` calls. On page 3 the 43 holds alternate strictly between the agent and the filer. The agent always waits for the boundary when the filer calls, so each filer call ends after one turn or one chunk and pays the fixed cost of a call again: the filer's held milliseconds per item rose from 66 to 101, 155 and 182 by page, the agent's from 52 to 68, 96 and 120. The alternation costs each of them a call's fixed cost at every turn, and it follows from this change's yield rules: a call that a waiting writer interrupts ends after a turn or a chunk. The rule: an agent's `file` call (`agent_filing` in `commands.py`) lists the stage with the filer as before, and the filer, when it comes to the stage (`Filer.run_once`), calls nothing while an agent's call runs or ended less than `AGENT_QUIET_SECONDS` ago. The window is twice the mutation lock's limit of a hold (`mutation_lock.DEFAULT_LONG_HOLDER_SECONDS`, 60 s): a call ends inside one limit, and an agent that reads the reply and calls again does so inside a second. The filer reschedules the stage for the end of the window and looks again, and an agent's later call moves the end. A reply that is `done` or `stopped` leaves the agent nothing to call again, so the filer is woken to record it at once. A `file` call with `wait` false is the agent's hand-off to the cell: `wake` ends the window, so the filer files the stage at once and not after what the agent's earlier calls left. A stage that stands aside is not a stall: its `mark`, `unmoved` and `stalls` stay as they are and it asks for no support. The record stays `working`, with no stop and no need: the stage is not halted and no one has to act, and a `parked` record would say the opposite to a reader of the filing view and of the shipped skill. The filing view adds a `note` (`AGENT_FILING_NOTE`) while the filer's memory says an agent files the stage, which ends by itself with the window, so the wait writes no record and a record never claims it for good. Each reply of the agent's call writes its counts into the record's `progress` with a `rate` sample, one durable write after the one that lists the stage, so counts and `as_of` move together instead of showing the filer's last call. The agent's calls are known in memory, in the one process that serves the cell and its filer. The Cloud CLI's driver calls `adoption_studio file` through the cell's write door (`cloud_import_windows.py`), a call that runs in the cell's process, so it is seen. Only a local exomem process that writes a vault whose local server runs a filer is not seen: the filer keeps its present behaviour for it, and a restart forgets the window and the filer files at once. The window of twice the limit assumes that a model-driven agent reads a reply and calls again within one hold limit. That holds for the scripted agent of the rehearsals and is not measured for a model. A miss costs at most one filer call, which the agent that returns then waits through as it did before this paragraph. Estimate, not measured: page 3 would lose the filer's 143 s of holds, and the agent alone would file its 5,611 items at about 96 ms each in about 540 s, against 593 s with the filer. The filer's own `file` call is no agent. Before this paragraph it listed its stage again through the same command (`note_file_call`), which reset the stage's due time and its count of busy tries, so a delay that a call returned (the stall back-off, the free-space check) was dropped and the stage was due again at once in the serving runtime; the tests drove an unstarted filer, which the command does not find, and did not show it. `agent_filing` skips a caller that runs as background work.

Rejected: a per-loop deadline constant (a second copy of the limit in each loop); leaving a `finish` that meets a waiter or a short budget as a final call (it would strand staged copies as `unchecked` for a person to review); a narrowed copy of the budget that only the step's own loops read (nested readers would run to the request's full budget); a fixed minimum slice for every waiter, whatever the step (it lengthens an interactive writer's wait by the slice, and `apply` already has its chunk); counting the calls of the import itself as non-interactive waiters (an agent's `file` call is a foreground writer and the rule must not pick which one yields); a reply to a `file` call that has no run, to carry an export's reading (the dispatcher replays a reply that repeats the arguments, so a continuation needs a run, which the first call does not have).

**What stays unbounded.** These pieces run to their end whatever room is left. The costs were measured with probes of the same code on a generated vault, on a loaded laptop (other sessions were running), unless marked as an estimate. The probe commands are in the PR body.

- `start` and `rescan`: each has a hold of its own. A scan step runs only as the first step of a call, and a call that read an app export ends before it scans. The first call of a stage finds its exports before it scans, a walk of the stage: 1.05 s for 70,000 staged files (measured on a generated stage). A `rescan` walks the stage again (`rewalk=True`) in the same hold as its scan, so it also pays that walk. The first page of a scan summarises the whole stage with `overview` (collection store ownership read), which reads each staged note under the vault's read guards. Measured on first pages: 3.6 s for 5,000 files and 16.7 s for 20,000 files, so about 0.7 to 0.8 ms per staged file. Measured at the rehearsal's size (run 45941473643, 69,300 staged notes): the one agent `start` call took 20.1 s and held the boundary for 19.9 s (journal row of 05:05:18Z), about 0.29 ms per staged file and not the 0.7 to 0.8 ms of the probes above. The scenario's agent made only `start` and `file` calls, so no `rescan` was measured. A `rescan` pays the overview and the export walk again, so about 21 s by that rate (an estimate). By the measured rate the scan alone would pass the 30 s limit from about 100,000 staged files (an estimate). A keyed write that arrives during the scan is not refused while the hold is inside 30 s: it retries for up to 40 s. Once the hold passes 30 s, a keyed write is refused with the cause `holder_overdue` at the end of its current 5 s attempt, and an unkeyed REST write is refused after its one 5 s wait (D1a, "Who is refused"). The scan is not the residual of this change at 69,300 files; past about 100,000 files it would be, and it is stated here, not fixed. Later pages of the same scan cost 0.46 s at 20,000 files, because the run keeps the overview. A `rescan` is a new scan, so it pays the first page's cost again. The transcripts that expansion derives from an export are staged files, so they count toward the overview; the page cap does not bound them (a page selects at most 5,000 files, and the overview covers the stage). Only the expansion's own cap of 65,536 records (`archive_members.MAX_ENTRIES`) bounds them. `overview` is shared with the collection overview, has no stop and is not changed here. Bounding it needs a resumable overview for import stages, which is the next change of this programme, together with the warm-walk cost above. Until it ships, task 4.0 requires no livelock (no call refused 30 times) and no refusal of any writer with `holder_overdue` or `capture_wait_exhausted`, and it reports the throughput of the import against its deadline without failing on it (task 4.0). It reports two counts, which the report reads as follows. The agent's own refused calls, by the cause the cell named: `holder_overdue` is a hold past 30 s that a keyed write met (a scan, a warm walk, a chunk or a plan call past the limit), and `capture_wait_exhausted` is a 40 s wait that a busy but not overdue boundary outlasted; the longest wait comes with them. The mutation journal's `MUTATION_BUSY` rows by command, which name no cause and include every unkeyed REST write and the filer's refusals; a filer row is `holder_overdue` or `capture_wait_exhausted` (D1a, "Who is refused"), read against the cell log, and counts as a defect like an agent's. At 69,300 files the scan (19.9 s) and the warm walk (about 6 s projected) are inside the 30 s limit, so a `holder_overdue` refusal there comes from a chunk, a `plan` call or the code after an item loop, and is a defect of this change. Past about 100,000 files a scan that outruns the limit is the residual named below. After `start` the `file` chain finds its budget spent and starts no further step.
- `select`: it classifies one page of at most 5,000 rows, 0.09 s measured for 1,000 rows. `MAX_CANDIDATES_DEFAULT` in `adoption_run.py` sets that page size; `file` passes it to `start` as `max_candidates`. No upload window, CLI setting or Cloud service sets it.
- `finish`'s tail (recall check, coverage, empty folders, residual, owed rows, manifest) runs alone in the call after the removals, so it has the hold's whole room, and each of its searches starts only while the reserve is left. Measured at 5,000 filed items, in a bare process on a generated vault of 10,000 pages (5,000 notes and 5,000 Sources) that has no maintained search index and binds no budget: the removals took 5.8 s in call 1, and the tail took 92.3 s in call 2. The recall sample took 84.2 s (ten `find` calls, about 8.4 s each), the recall check 6.9 s (one `find`), the manifest write 0.8 s, and the empty-folder pass 0.04 s. A direct probe of `find` on that vault took 11.4 s for the first call, which raised `RETRIEVAL_INDEX_WARMING`, and 13.6 s and 7.9 s for the next two. Earlier runs gave 5.4 s at 300 items and 16.3 s at 1,000 items. That measurement was taken before the tail checked its room. A `find` on a cold index raised `RETRIEVAL_INDEX_WARMING` in 2 to 8 s whatever the bound budget said, and the sample started all ten, so the tail held the boundary for about 90 s. The tail now starts a search only while the budget covers the delivery reserve, and it ends the sample at the first search that the index cannot answer yet. The recall check, which runs first, treats warming the same way: its answer is `pending` with what is warming, not `failed`, and when it is `pending` the sample starts no search at all. The searches that the sample did not reach count as `pending`, never as found or missed. At most one `find` can therefore run past the room, which the probes of a cold index put at 8 to 14 s; the test `test_a_finish_whose_search_index_is_warming_answers_pending_after_one_search_and_not_failed` proves that bound with a warming index, and the tail's seconds on a cold index are not measured again after the change. The cell's service keeps its index maintained, which the measurement did not cover; the rehearsal of this branch records the `finish` call's seconds on the cell. Deferring the tail to its own call does not shorten it. It stops the removals from spending the room that the checks need.
- `Destinations.frozen_by` and `seen_in_plan` of `plan` walk the run's draft once per call: linear in the page's items, at most 5,000.
- `resolve_links` sorts the whole index once, at the plan's last call.
- The run object is saved whole once per call, so a call that ends often saves it often. It grows with the page, not the vault.

### D1b. The Sources index that `plan` reads is remembered per process

A plan read every Source page of the vault at each call, to find the earlier imports of its originals: the cost of a call grew with the Sources already filed, so page 40 of a large import planned slower than page 1 for the same files. `adoption_plan._SOURCE_PAGES` now remembers what each page recorded.

- **What is cached.** For each vault, keyed by its real path, the entries of each Source page (`EarlierImport` values for its original and each `also_imported_from` path) with the file signature the page had when it was read. `ImportIndex` then keys those entries by label and by original path, so a lookup for one original does not test every other Source.
- **Invalidation.** A page is read again when its signature (mtime, ctime, size, inode) differs from the remembered one: the same rule as the note index (`NoteIndex`). A page that changed less than two seconds before the read (`_RACY_NS`) is remembered without a signature, so the next pass reads it again; a timestamp cannot prove a later change inside that window. A pass that reaches every page replaces the vault's map, so a deleted page leaves it. A pass that a stop ended keeps what the vault's map held for the pages it did not reach.
- **Memory.** About 1.6 KB for a Source with one entry, more as `also_imported_from` grows, held for the life of the process. At 70,000 Sources that is about 110 MB (estimate from 1.6 KB per entry; not measured at that size). The map holds at most 4 vaults (`_KNOWN_VAULTS`) and drops the least recently used one.
- **Cost.** A warmed cold read of a Source page costs about 0.47 ms in the critic's measurement and 0.4 ms in ours (2.0 to 2.5 s for 5,000 small generated Sources). A warm read costs one `lstat` per page: 59 to 63 ms for 5,000 Sources on the reference machine, 0.31 s on the CI runner of the rehearsal (run 45941473643, the second plan call of page 2), and 1.04 s and 1.39 s at 10,000 and 15,000 Sources. A plan call after a process start pays the cold read once. With `INDEX_READ_FLOOR` and the stop of D1a, a vault larger than the floor is read over several calls.
- **Correctness.** The cache decides nothing that the page does not: a lookup runs the same predicate over the entries, and the disk is never written from it. `apply` reads the same cached index when its Sources seal broke, so a stale entry could let it file a duplicate. The signature rule, with the racy window, is what rules that out: a changed page is read again, and a page too new to trust is read at every pass. A Source page that the filesystem refuses for now (`CAPABILITY_UNAVAILABLE`, which a filer call meets while an identity-catalogue build is in flight, and `IDENTITY_CHANGED`; the closed set `_REFUSED_FOR_NOW`) is not a page that records no imports. A read that has a stop (the plan's, and the full read in `apply`) ends incomplete on such a refusal and leaves the page unremembered, so the next call reads it again; a plan never completes on a read that skipped a page it could not see. A persistent refusal gives the plan no progress per call, and the filing stall limit (`adoption_run.FILING_STALL_CALLS`, a `working` reply that repeats the counts and page position of the reply before it, 5 times in a row, one value for both drivers) ends the run of calls and names it. The Cloud CLI's driver stops with the reply's code and reason and leaves the stage staged on the cell; it asks for no support. The cell's filer does not park a stalled stage for good: it parks it in a back-off, with the visible stop `IMPORT_FILING_STALLED` ("filing made no progress in N calls; it will try again") and no need, and calls again after 60 s, then 120 s, doubling to a cap of 15 minutes (`STALL_FIRST_BACKOFF_SECONDS`, `STALL_MAX_BACKOFF_SECONDS`). A stall is not a fault the person can mend, so it asks for nobody. An agent's own `file` call lists the stage again with its full count of calls. The limit reads the reply's counts and its page's position, so real progress must show in that position. A read of a cold Sources index spans many calls for a vault of more than 10,000 Sources (2,000 pages per call) under a stream of waiting writers, and a `finish` whose removals span more than five calls does the same. The position therefore carries `sources_read` while the plan's read is stopped part-way (the count of Source pages this process has read and remembered, by `plan` or `apply`) and `copies_settled` while a `finish` removes copies, so neither shows as a stall. A stage whose calls change no count and no such position still shows no movement, and it backs off. A new process starts with an empty index cache: nothing in this design warms it for the next start of the cell, so the first plan after a restart reads the whole index again. Callers that pass no stop (`adopt.py`, `original_fixity.py`, the whole-index read in `apply`, `read_import_entries`) keep reading a refused page as one with no entries, as before. They run outside the filer's import chain, and the same trigger (an identity-catalogue build) can still make one of them miss an earlier import. That is old debt, not changed here.

### D2. A waiter counts only when it is interactive

`waiter_count()` counts threads of the process that wait with an interactive holder kind, plus the keyed writes queued behind the head of the capture line, plus a waiter in another process. Machine holder kinds and `maintenance-job` waiters are excluded, so background work does not yield to other background work. A borrower of a lent boundary waits for a loan and is excluded. The thread that polls the boundary's lock file raises the cross-process signal while it is interactive or while an interactive writer waits behind it in the process, re-evaluated at each poll, so a writer queued behind a local machine poller is still seen by a holder in another process.

The head ticket of the capture line stays in the line while its write runs, so the count starts behind the head. The head's own gaps between attempts are not visible to the count; its attempts already poll the boundary every 50 ms.

### D3. A hold that ended for a waiter hands the boundary over

A writer polls for the boundary every 25 ms. A holder that asks again at once wins the race. After a hold that ended while a writer waited, `hand_over()` waits while `waiter_count() > 0`, polling at the writer's rate, and returns at the cap of `foreground_priority.MAX_YIELD_SECONDS`, or earlier at the pass's progress share. A thread that holds a boundary never waits. A foreground thread does wait, because the wait is on a writer, not on itself, and the cap bounds it. A thread inside `ending_waits`, the collection store thread, skips the wait, because its waiter may need it.

The wait holds no lock. It can lengthen a job by at most the cap per hold; it cannot lengthen any writer's wait.

### D4. Reconcile phases that grow with the vault hold in batches

The unit-sidecar phase and the embedding phase ran one hold over every item. They now run through `apply_in_holds`: a hold takes batches of items and ends after `HOLD_SECONDS`, when a writer waits, or when a stop is requested. Each batch re-reads under the hold what it applies, as before. The phase hold labels are single tokens (`reconcile_units`), because the holder label sanitiser maps a label with a space to `unknown`.

The batches cost the job time. Every hold ends with a commit-generation bump, so readers re-validate their cached stamps, and a phase now bumps once per second of work, plus once per early end for a waiting writer, instead of once. The embedding phase refreshed every drifted page in one call; it now calls the refresh once per batch of 4 pages, so the fixed cost of each call repeats. The cost buys writer latency.

The other phases keep one hold. Their work is bounded by a constant or by seconds: the manifest, lifecycle cleanup, indexes, the graph build's own flight, derived-receipt batches (at most 256), references and receipts. They are reported as debt, not changed here.

### D5. Windows keeps a process-local signal

`msvcrt.locking` offers an exclusive lock only. A shared lock needs `LockFileEx` through `ctypes`, which this change cannot test. Windows holders see waiters in their own process, as before.

### D6. Replay marker and digest

`_replay` returns the stored terminal with `replayed: true`. `mutated`, `request_id` and `receipt_id` stay those of the original commit, so the reply names the write it replays. The marker leaves `mutated` alone because the egress filter binds the parent of a confined unit write on it. The implicit digest hashes the arguments with every string in Unicode NFC. FastMCP has already applied schema defaults to the arguments, so they need no second pass. The window stays 600 s, and the spec is corrected.

Skipped: `repeat_of`, a field that lets an agent repeat a write on purpose. An agent that repeats on purpose changes the arguments or waits out the window, and the field adds a schema entry to every write tool.

## Risks

- An import under a steady stream of writes returns one chunk, a turn of planned items (`TURN_SECONDS`, 1 s) or a turn of removed copies per call. Each call makes progress, so the import finishes. Two importers that file one stage (an agent and the cell's filer, or two agents) both move its page on at every call and both finish it. The two-importer path at the size where the livelock showed is evidenced by run 45941473643 (four pages, the agent and the filer in strict alternation, no livelock) and by the scaled two-importer tests (task 3.4). The new rehearsal does not repeat it: with the filer standing aside the scripted agent is the one importer, so a pass of task 4.0 proves the single importer at size and the filer's yielding, not the livelock fix for two importers. The filer retries a busy boundary as a keyed write does, and a refusal of it (`holder_overdue`, `capture_wait_exhausted`) is a defect that task 4.0 counts; it backs off and tries again. A stage whose `working` replies repeat the reply before them 5 times in a row backs off with a visible reason, never for good, and tries again after a delay of 1 to 15 minutes; a read of the Sources or a run of removals that moves a count at each call is not a stall.
- A call for a stage with app exports takes about two calls more and one scan more than a stage without them, and a `finish` that removed staged text takes one call more for its checks.
- The handoff wait delays a maintenance job by at most the cap per release, and only while a writer waits.
- A writer in another process that never gets served keeps the signal for its wait bound; the handoff wait ends at the cap.

## Known residuals

- A `start` or `rescan` holds the boundary for as long as its scan takes: 19.9 s for `start` at 69,300 staged files in run 45941473643 (journal row of 05:05:18Z, hold 19,940 ms). It passes the 30 s limit at about 100,000 files (an estimate by that rate), and a `rescan` would cost more than `start` (not measured). A writer that arrives past 30 s can be refused (D1a, "Who is refused"). The next change of this programme, a resumable overview for import stages, removes it.
- The warm walk of the Sources index has no stop. Its cost is the second `plan` call's `adoption.plan_index` span: 0.31, 1.04 and 1.39 s at 5,000, 10,000 and 15,000 Sources in run 45941473643, about 6 s projected at 69,300 (an estimate). It is inside the 30 s limit at the rehearsal's size, so it is not what refused a writer there. The first `plan` call of a page reads the previous page's Sources cold (9.8, 3.6 and 4.3 s), which has the floor and a stop. The resumable read in the follow-up change removes the walk's growth.
- The refusal of an agent `file` call at 05:35:23.8 in that run (`MUTATION_BUSY`, the only journal row of that code) is unattributed. The hold that refused it was the cell filer's call, request 54f40428: a gap of 19.2 s between its two one-item commits, then a first chunk of 256 items at a hold age of 27 s. The likeliest cause is the oversized first chunk under a waiting writer, which this change bounds; that is not proven, because the filer's calls leave no span and the event `adoption_apply_opening` did not exist. The rehearsal of this branch counts refused writes by cause and the event names the opening's costs.
- A `plan` call held the boundary for 28.2 s once in that run (05:27:45 UTC, page 4: `plan_items` 19.7 s, of which `plan_duplicates` 13.5 s, `plan_read` 3.8 s, `plan_index` 1.4 s). That is inside the 30 s limit by 1.8 s. A `plan` call that holds past the limit and refuses a writer is a defect of this change, not the walk; the per-item cost behind it is the next bullet.
- A `plan` item costs more as the vault holds more Sources: 1.3, 2.4, 6.0 and 7.0 ms per item on pages 1 to 4 of that run. The duplicate lookup is most of it (`adoption.plan_duplicates` 0.7 to 13.5 s of a `plan_items` span of 2.3 to 19.7 s). One cause is the per-item connection pair that a lookup opens while a whole-catalogue rebuild is in flight (`lexstore.shared_reads` falls back to it); that is old debt, not changed here. Held-boundary seconds per filed item grew in step: pages 1 to 4 filed 18.0, 13.1, 9.2 and 7.5 items per held second.
- A chunk is one commit and has no stop inside it. Its cost per item varied from a median of about 10 ms to about 80 ms in the worst chunk, and the pacer prices from what it has timed, so a chunk can still outrun its estimate by a few times. The growth cap and the one-turn first chunk bound the estimate's error; they do not bound the commit. At 69,300 files a chunk that outruns the limit is the likeliest source of a `holder_overdue` refusal, so it is a defect for task 4.0 when it refuses a writer, and this residual records the risk that remains, not an acceptance of the refusal. The 30.7 s hold of 05:28:20 (a 253-item chunk, before the growth cap) is the instance.
- The cell filer's own calls leave no span in the call ledger, so a filer hold is read from the mutation journal (`scope` owner) and the cell log, not from its spans. An `apply` call, the filer's included, writes the event `adoption_apply_opening` to the cell log with the seconds of its read of the index, its first chunk's size, prework and commit seconds, and whether a writer waited. It does not time the work before the read of the index (the settle loop and the intent commit).
- The import's throughput is a residual of the follow-up change. Run 45941473643 filed pages 1 to 4 in 279, 408, 593 and 489 s of `file` calls (5,000 files each on pages 1 to 3, 3,303 files on page 4 when the run ended): 17.9, 12.3, 8.4 and 6.8 files per second. At the overall 10.0 files per second the 69,300 files need about 6,900 s, and about 5,650 s of the 115-minute deadline (counted from the run's start, `started` in `_run`) remained when paging began, so the import would miss it, and the rate was still falling. The per-call means of the commit spans cannot show where the time went, because they are flat by construction: a call ends inside the hold's room, and `derived.post_canonical` runs from the canonical commit to the end of the acknowledgement (`writer_lease.py`), so it includes the chunk loop. The per-item numbers show it. By page, the commits (204, 148, 198 and 141 of them, with 28, 37, 28 and 27 items on average) cost 11.2, 17.2, 21.7 and 24.6 ms per item, and the agent's `derived.canonical_commit` seconds totalled 61, 70, 107 and 85 s (page 4 partial); a `plan` item cost 1.3, 2.4, 6.0 and 7.0 ms (above); the prework of a call (`adoption.prework`) cost about 2.7, 4.0, 7.7 and 9.4 ms per item filed (the span's mean per call over the items per call, an estimate), and an agent call filed about 365, 292, 213 and 180 items. So the per-item cost of the plan, the prework and the commit grows with the vault; that is old debt and the follow-up change's. The calls per page rose (13, 15, 22 and 18) because each call, capped to the hold's room, fits fewer items, not because of the turn rule: no interactive writer waited on an agent's hold in this scenario. The alternation of the filer with the agent was this change's own cost, and "The filer stands aside from a stage that an agent files" removes it.
- Within 24 hours of the upgrade, a retry under an explicit key whose text is not in Unicode NFC gets `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED`. The receipt stored before the upgrade holds a digest of the text as sent, and the new digest normalises it first. A retry under a new key commits.
- An import apply interrupted before the upgrade, with paths in NFD, gets `IMPORT_RECOVERY_UNRESOLVED` on resume, for the same reason: its stored request digest predates the normalisation.
