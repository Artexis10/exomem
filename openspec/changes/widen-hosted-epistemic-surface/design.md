## Context

`PRODUCT_SURFACE_PROFILES` in `src/exomem/commands.py` is the single declarative source of Hosted profile membership. It holds two entries today:

- `hosted-alpha-agent-v1` — thirteen commands: `bootstrap`, `ask_memory`, `read_memory`, `browse_memory`, `remember`, `observe_memory`, `capture_source`, `compile_source`, `preserve_evidence`, `review_memory`, `review_item_context`, `triage_memory`, `connect_memory`.
- `hosted-alpha-agent-v2` — those thirteen plus `record_memory`.

Neither exposes `replace_memory` (supersession), `plan_memory` (intent), or `edit_memory` (in-place correction). All three are Tier-1 product commands already available on the `cli`, `mcp`, and `rest` surfaces, so `product_commands_for_profile` can resolve them for a Hosted profile with no registry change.

The v1 -> v2 `record_memory` addition is the precedent this change follows. It did not mutate v1: it added a second profile constant, a second candidate directory under `plugins/hosted/candidates/`, a second generated tree under `plugins/hosted/generated/candidates/`, and a candidate-scoped skill. Every v1 artifact and its recorded release identity stayed byte-identical, which is why `tests/fixtures/hosted/v1-release-identities.json` still pins the v1 chain.

`hosted_plugins.py` reached that second candidate by branching on `candidate == LIFECYCLE_CANDIDATE` in roughly a dozen places. A third candidate makes that shape untenable, so this change replaces the pairwise branching with a candidate registry and a `RECORDS_CANDIDATES` predicate.

## Goals / Non-Goals

Goals:

- Give the Hosted tier a complete epistemic loop: capture, recall, review, connect, record, **revise**, **plan**, and **correct**.
- Keep v1 and v2 provably frozen — membership, order, generated bytes, locks, and promotion records.
- Keep profile membership strictly separate from the full local tool surface, so no ChatGPT Personal Plugin fingerprint moves as a side effect.
- Make the candidate plumbing extensible rather than pairwise.

Non-Goals:

- Promoting `hosted-alpha-agent-v3` to `live`. Promotion needs signed live acceptance evidence; the committed promotion records stay `pending`, exactly as v2's did when v2 landed.
- Changing the deployment lock's active profile. `infra/contracts/exomem-hosted-deployment-lock-v3.schema.json` still pins `hosted-alpha-agent-v2` and this change does not touch it.
- Any change to the behaviour, arguments, or discovery schema of `replace_memory`, `plan_memory`, or `edit_memory`.
- Rendering an OpenAI package for v3 (see the `edit_memory` decision below).

## Decisions

### Decision 1: v3 is v2 plus three commands, appended in that order

`hosted-alpha-agent-v3` is `hosted-alpha-agent-v2`'s fourteen commands in their pinned order, followed by `replace_memory`, `plan_memory`, `edit_memory`. Making v3 a superset of v2 rather than of v1 avoids a fourth axis (a "records-less epistemic" profile nobody asked for) and keeps `compatibility.json["commands"]` for v3 a literal prefix-extension of v2's, which is cheap to assert in a test.

Appending rather than interleaving matters because `command_surface_sha256` is order-sensitive; a prefix-preserving order makes "v3 did not disturb v2" checkable by list slicing.

### Decision 2: include `edit_memory`, and take the Claude-surface-first half of the condition

The audit adjudicated all three commands as *include*, but attached a condition to `edit_memory`: prefer a Claude-surface Hosted rollout first, **or** admit only compact discriminated-`operation` forms, because of the recorded ChatGPT overloaded-schema friction failure of 2026-08-11.

**Decision: include `edit_memory` unchanged, and satisfy the condition by channel — render and commit the v3 candidate for the `claude` platform only.**

The schema half of the condition is *not* available to us, and an earlier draft of this document was wrong to claim it. `edit_memory`'s top-level property count is small — five: `path`, `why`, `operation`, `validate_only`, `response_detail` — but that count hides the payload. Measured from `tests/fixtures/mcp_tool_schemas.json`, `edit_memory`'s full `inputSchema` is **9,274 characters**, the widest in the addition set and 85% wider than `record_memory` (5,002), against `replace_memory` at 7,433 and `plan_memory` at 3,096. Its `operation` `oneOf` alone is 8,524 characters across seven variants. Whatever "compact" means for an action-schema cache, a tool that is simultaneously the narrowest at the top level and the largest overall cannot be argued to be compact on the strength of the first fact. So the discriminated-`operation` form is a genuine readability improvement for the model, and it is *not* evidence about ChatGPT ingestion cost.

That leaves the channel half, which is defensible on its own terms and is what this change takes. The 2026-08-11 record establishes that a changed action surface needs its own post-deploy ChatGPT refresh and its own acceptance run, and that reconnecting OAuth is not such a refresh. Emitting a third OpenAI package alongside the widening would create exactly that obligation while nothing in this change discharges it.

So v3 ships `plugins/hosted/generated/candidates/hosted-alpha-agent-v3/claude*` and no `openai*` artifacts.

Two honest qualifications about how strong that control is:

- It is **deferred and tripwired, not fail-closed.** `render(platform="all", candidate=v3)` produces a complete, valid OpenAI package today; nothing in the code refuses it. And `promote(candidate=v3, platform="openai")` fails for the ordinary reason that no generated lock exists — identically to `promote(candidate=v3, platform="claude")`, with no OpenAI-specific behaviour. What actually holds the line is the committed absence of those artifacts plus `test_v3_ships_no_openai_package_until_a_chatgpt_refresh_is_accepted`, which fails CI the moment they appear. That is a real tripwire and worth having, but it is a review gate, not an enforcement mechanism.
- Extending v3 to the OpenAI channel is a separate change with its own acceptance evidence, and should probably also carry a measured answer to whether a 9 KB `oneOf` survives the ChatGPT action cache.

A rejected alternative: shipping `edit_memory` on v3 in a further-narrowed "hosted-only" schema variant. Rejected because it would fork a tool's discovery schema by surface, which is exactly the drift `hosted-gateway-contract`'s "Forwarded commands preserve the registry contract" requirement exists to prevent — the gateway must forward registry-identical names, schemas, and read/write metadata.

### Decision 2a: the protection v1 got from absence has to become a real control

**This supersedes a requirement in another change, and the mechanism that hid the conflict is worth naming.**

`openspec/changes/add-hosted-agent-surface-profile/specs/hosted-agent-surface/spec.md` — the change that created `hosted-alpha-agent-v1` — contains the requirement *"Hosted Alpha Agent Profile Is Explicit And Least Privilege"*, whose third scenario reads:

> **WHEN** an agent requests `edit_memory` or `replace_memory`, including with a path under `_Schema`
> **THEN** the command is absent from the profile and rejected before invocation or lifecycle admission
> **AND** governed semantic-unit mutation remains available through `observe_memory`

That protection was provided *entirely by profile absence*. v3 removes the absence, so it removes the protection.

`openspec validate --strict` could not see the conflict, and this is a structural gap rather than bad luck: `hosted-agent-surface` has **no entry under `openspec/specs/`**. It exists only inside the never-archived `add-hosted-agent-surface-profile` change, so there is no main-spec requirement for a `## MODIFIED Requirements` delta to target and no cross-change conflict detection to trip. A reviewer reading only the validated artifacts would not have found it. The same is true of `command-surface`'s v2 profile requirement, which lives in the equally unarchived `make-records-first-class-and-recoverable`.

**Decision: re-establish the control in code, at the hosted mutation boundary, and state it normatively in this change's `hosted-agent-surface` delta.**

Verified by probe through the real hosted forwarding route before the fix — trusted-context auth, argument coercion, profile resolution, lifecycle admission, real command leaf — both writes committed:

```
POST /private/exomem/v1/agent/hosted-alpha-agent-v3/command/edit_memory
  path=Knowledge Base/_Schema/SKILL.md                  -> 200 {"mutated": true}
  path=Knowledge Base/_Schema/references/page-types.md  -> 200 {"mutated": true}
```

`_Schema/references/frontmatter.md` and `page-types.md` are what `schema.py` loads as *the* frontmatter and page-type contract, so this was an unguarded write primitive over the tenant's own governing doctrine — on the tier with the least out-of-band supervision, where a prompt-injected `capture_source` is a sufficient delivery vector.

**`_Governance` needed the same treatment, and now has it.** Probed separately: `edit_memory` against `Knowledge Base/_Governance/README.md` also committed. `is_governance_path` guards *reads* (`get`, `fetch`, `overview`, the governance tool) and nothing guards writes. Both trees are covered.

**`observe_memory` did not need it.** The v1 requirement's claim that governed mutation "remains available through `observe_memory`" holds: probed against `_Schema/SKILL.md` it refuses with `OBSERVE_TARGET_NOT_WRITABLE_COMPILED_PAGE`, because the tree contains no writable compiled page. That is why the guard covers only the commands v3 adds.

**Why the hosted mutation boundary and not the write leaf.** A single-user local vault legitimately customises its own `_Schema` — that is the documented way to adapt page types and frontmatter — so a blanket refusal at the leaf would break the local product to fix a hosted problem. The property being enforced is *"this surface does not get this authority"*, not *"this operation is unsafe"*, and the hosted command route is the layer that already knows the active surface profile, already refuses `transfer_artifact` and `adopt_vault`, and already gates `record_memory` lifecycle actions. The guard sits with them: after coercion, before lifecycle admission and before the leaf, which is also the disposition the v1 requirement asked for ("rejected before invocation or lifecycle admission").

**Three bypasses, one cause.** This guard was bypassed three times by three different reviewers, and the enumerate-the-next-shape response failed each time. The shapes were unrelated; the cause was not.

| Round | Probe that got through | What the guard read | What the executor read |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | `Knowledge Base/_Schema/SKILL.md` | nothing — no guard existed, protection came from profile absence | the file |
| 3 | `/Knowledge Base/_Schema/SKILL.md` | "absolute, resolves nowhere in the vault, not mine" | rooted-looking, so vault-relative — the file |
| 4 | `_GOVER~1/README.md` | vault root + text → a path that does not exist, so resolution was a no-op | KB root + text → `_Governance/README.md`, 1287 bytes → 18 |

Every row is the same defect: **a guard and an executor holding independent notions of the same path.** Round 3's fix — evaluate more readings, never fail open — was correct as far as it went and still did not help in round 4, because it added readings to a normaliser that was still the guard's own. `edit._resolve` prepends `kb_prefix()` when the caller omits it, which the tool description explicitly invites; the guard joined at the vault root. The two functions could not agree, so no list of shapes was ever going to be long enough.

**The fix is to delete the second normaliser.** `kbdir.kb_relative_form` / `kb_page_target` now hold the rel computation that `edit._resolve` and `replace._resolve_kb_path` had byte-identical copies of — strip, normalise separators, drop leading separators, supply the KB prefix, supply `.md`. Both leaves call it. The guard calls it. `test_guard_and_page_leaves_share_one_normaliser` asserts the identity of the function object in both leaves and greps their sources for a re-implementation, so the next person to inline it fails a test rather than opens round five. This is why the fix is not "handle `_GOVER~1`": handling `_GOVER~1` leaves the divergence in place.

One leaf's normalisation was **not** extracted, and that is worth stating rather than glossing. The collection commands (`plan_memory`, `record_memory`) normalise inside `structured_collections.parse_manifest_bytes`, where the two lines that join a relative manifest path to the vault root are interleaved with validation that raises typed `CollectionError`s — there is no separable rel computation to lift out without restructuring the parser's error contract. What the guard reproduces there is the join itself (`vault_root / path`), not a normaliser: one line, no prefix logic, no suffix logic. The guard also evaluates the page-leaf rel-form for the same argument, so it over-approximates rather than under-approximates. The residual is that if `structured_collections` ever changes its join root, the guard would diverge for `plan_memory` — bounded by `plan_memory`'s guard being defence in depth over `_require_profile_layer` rather than the only control, and recorded here so it is a known edge rather than a surprise.

**Two readings, both platform-correct, both fail-closed.** Over each candidate target the guard applies a *spelling* reading and a *resolution* reading, and any hit refuses.

The spelling reading is per component, case-folded, and additionally strips trailing dots and spaces, because Windows strips them before the filesystem sees the component — `_Schema ` and `_Schema.` both open `_Schema`, and the earlier guard compared the component as typed. It runs under both `PurePosixPath` and `PureWindowsPath` rather than Posix alone: `C:_Schema/x.md` is one opaque component to Posix and drive-relative on the platform this actually deploys to, and the same change covers UNC and `\\?\` spellings without special-casing them.

The resolution reading resolves the **deepest existing ancestor** of the target and tests containment against the resolved protected roots, then applies the spelling reading to the unresolved remainder. The deepest-ancestor part is what makes it work at all: `resolve()` cannot canonicalise a component that is not on disk, and the interesting target usually is not — creating a *new* governing document is the normal way doctrine would be extended, and a resolution-only guard sees nothing there. `test_guard_refuses_a_page_that_does_not_exist_yet` pins that case across all guarded commands.

Fail-closed is now a property of the whole evaluation rather than of the arms someone remembered: `_names_a_protected_tree` wraps everything and returns `True` on any exception. `test_guard_refuses_when_a_reading_raises` passes an object whose `__str__` raises. **A guard's unknown case is "refuse", never "allow."**

**Partial coverage of a guarded command is not coverage.** `plan_memory` selects by `collection` as well as `manifest_path`, and only the path-shaped one was guarded — a second caller-supplied target on a guarded command, which is precisely the shape the previous rounds kept re-creating. Both are guarded now. `replace_memory`'s `slug` names the successor page's filename and stays out on a real constraint rather than an omission: `vault.resolve_filename_slug` admits lowercase ASCII kebab-case only, which has no `/`, `.` or `_` to spell a tree with, and `INVALID_SLUG` is what a probe gets.

The differential test still pins the other direction — 26 refusals against 14 lookalikes (`_Schemas/`, `_Schema_backup/`, `my_Schema/`, `about_Schema-design.md`, `Notes/schema.md`, `Notes/Governance.md`, a UNC path to `Notes/`) — so "refuse everything" cannot pass for a fix.

All three bypasses were latent, not live: no shipped deployment serves a v3 profile, and the candidate is unpromotable.

**What is proven here and what is not — the 8.3 case.** NTFS generates an 8.3 alias for `_Governance` (11 characters) and none for `_Schema` (7). That aliasing cannot be *generated* on this machine: the worktree is ext4 under WSL and Windows interop is blocked by a user-scope hook, which was not routed around. So the alias is stood up as a symlink, which gives `Path.resolve()` the same expansion semantics, and the tests assert the **403 from the guard** rather than merely that the write failed.

That distinction matters here more than usual, because on Linux the symlink stand-in is *incidentally* stopped by an unrelated control: before the fix, `_GOVER~1/README.md` came back `400 PATH_GUARD_UNSAFE` from `PathGuard`'s symlink check, not `200 mutated`. A real 8.3 alias is not a symlink, so `PathGuard` does not fire on Windows — which is why the reviewer's probe committed the write and this box's could not. **The write itself was reproduced on Windows by the reviewer and was not reproduced here.** What is proven here is every layer above the filesystem: the shared normaliser, the join root, the use of resolution, the deepest-ancestor split, and the hosted route. What is not proven here is NTFS.

`test_guard_refuses_a_native_ntfs_short_name_alias` closes that gap on the platform that can close it — it calls `GetShortPathNameW` for the real alias and skips elsewhere, the same convention `tests/test_windows_path_alias_guard.py` already uses for this exact problem. **Final confirmation of the 8.3 case requires a Windows run.** The trailing-dot and trailing-space cases have the same character: today they are stopped by an incidental "paths must be canonical" `ValueError` in `graph_sync.GraphSyncCheckpoint.create`, which is not a control and would vanish if that check were relaxed, so the guard now refuses them on its own.

**Fail-closed for the next widening.** The hole existed because nobody had to classify a profile's write surface. `assert_profile_mutations_are_classified` now runs at route registration and refuses to serve a profile with a mutating command that appears in neither the guarded-path map nor the explicitly target-constrained set, so a future v4 cannot reopen this by silence.

That classifier is necessary but not sufficient, and the docstring on `TARGET_CONSTRAINED_MUTATIONS` originally overclaimed by saying membership "is asserted, not assumed". The classifier only proves every mutating command is *named* somewhere; it cannot tell whether the claim attached to the name is true, so adding a name to the target-constrained set was a way to wave a command through. `test_target_constrained_mutations_are_actually_constrained` closes that.

Its first version closed it only halfway, and the half it missed is instructive. Filtering arguments on `"path" in key` certified `capture_source`, `preserve_evidence`, `remember` and `triage_memory` as taking no target-shaping argument — and all four take one. `preserve_evidence` composes its path from `scope`, `category` **and** `filename`; `capture_source` and `remember` derive a filename from `slug` and `title`; `triage_memory` selects by `ref`. Their leaves held, but the test was not the reason, which is the same "true by luck" the whole exercise is about.

So the filter is gone. Every **string-typed argument in the pinned schema** of every member is probed with a traversal aimed at the schema tree, and the assertion is the one that matters: both protected trees stay byte-identical and unchanged in membership, and the gateway guard never fires. Deriving the argument set from the schema rather than a hand list means a new argument is covered by default instead of certified by omission. Mutation-checked in both directions: adding `manage_memory_file` fails with `manage_memory_file takes {'path', 'new_path', 'old_path', 'trash_path', 'restore_path'} but is unproven`; misclassifying `preserve_evidence.filename` as guarded fails with `preserve_evidence.filename`, proving the "guard must not fire" half is live.

Two findings from the widened sweep are worth recording, because neither was obvious:

- **`preserve_evidence` returns `200 committed` for a traversal and is still safe.** With `filename` set to `../_Schema/injected-by-probe.md` it commits — to `Knowledge Base/Evidence/probe/probe/.._Schemainjected-by-probe.md`. Its leaf strips separators and dots from the component rather than rejecting it, so the traversal collapses into one literal filename that cannot leave `Evidence/`. Same for `scope` and `category`. That is a real constraint, but "200 committed" is not what a reader expects from a traversal probe, and it is exactly the kind of thing a `"path" in key` filter would never have surfaced.
- **`collection` is layer-constrained the same way `manifest_path` is.** `record_memory` and `plan_memory` both refuse `Knowledge Base/_Schema` with `INVALID_COLLECTION_PATH` from `_require_profile_layer`. `record_memory`'s stays out of the guarded map because it is published surface; `plan_memory`'s is now guarded because the command is new surface and a guarded command is guarded on every target argument.

The other results: `observe_memory` → `OBSERVE_TARGET_NOT_WRITABLE_COMPILED_PAGE`, `connect_memory` (`create-entity`) → `INVALID_LINK`, `record_memory` (`create`, with a valid manifest taken from its own published contract so it is refused on its *target* rather than bouncing off manifest validation) → `INVALID_COLLECTION_PATH`, `slug` on `remember` and `capture_source` → `INVALID_SLUG`. None is the guard's `HOSTED_PROTECTED_TREE_MUTATION`.

One honest limit: a probe whose other arguments cannot be made valid — a `triage_memory` `ref` has to name a real review item — bounces on argument validation before reaching placement. That still proves both assertions, but it does not exercise that leaf's placement logic, and the test says so rather than implying coverage it does not have.

**The rule distinguishing guarded `plan_memory.manifest_path` from target-constrained `record_memory.manifest_path`.** The reviewer was right that the parameter is identical and the leaf constraint is identical — `structured_collections._require_profile_layer` pins a manifest to its profile's `placement_layer`, `Records` or `Planning`, neither of which is a protected tree. So the distinction is not about the parameter, and the rule is now encoded rather than left to inspection: `PROTECTED_TREE_PATH_ARGUMENTS` is exactly `v3 - v2`, the commands this widening newly exposes, and already-published surface keeps its existing classification so no shipped profile changes behaviour. `test_guarded_set_is_exactly_what_the_widening_newly_exposes` asserts that set equality directly, and asserts the two sets are disjoint.

Guarding uniformly over the new surface means the guard is load-bearing for `edit_memory` and `replace_memory` and defence in depth for `plan_memory`, whose leaf would have refused anyway. `test_plan_memory_is_guarded_as_defence_in_depth_not_as_the_only_control` records that explicitly, so a later reader does not mistake uniform guarding for evidence that every guarded argument was independently exploitable.

**Residual, stated rather than hidden.** The legacy full private command route (`private-command-router`) still exposes the whole registry and is not covered by this guard. That is the pre-existing control-plane trust boundary, reached with the private service credential rather than by a tenant's agent, and this change neither widens nor narrows it.

### Decision 3: `replace_memory` is safe on Hosted because the guardrail is in the leaf, not the surface

The audit cites `relation_review.py` as the proposal-first guardrail that makes `replace_memory` safe to expose on the Hosted tier. Verified in code, and the claim holds for two independent reasons:

- **Predecessor binding.** `relation_review.py` treats `"replacement"` as a distinct operation and requires it to carry both a `predecessor_path` and a `predecessor_content_hash`; either missing, unsafe, or malformed raises `INVALID_PREDECESSOR`. Supplying predecessor fields on a non-replacement operation is likewise rejected. A supersession is therefore bound to the exact bytes the agent claims to have read, so a stale or hallucinated supersession cannot commit.
- **Proposal-first relation review.** A commit must either qualify structurally (the `bootstrap` or `qualifying_relation` dispositions) or carry an explicit `reviewed_none` disposition whose `relation_review_hash` equals the `draft_hash` from a *fresh* validation pass; a mismatch raises `DRAFT_HASH_MISMATCH`, and a commit that qualifies for neither path raises `INVALID_RELATION_REVIEW` ("reviewed-none approval is required"). The review artifact that results records the operation, the draft token hash, and the predecessor binding, so the approval cannot be replayed against different content.

Both checks live under the command leaf and the vault mutation boundary, not in any surface adapter. Exposing `replace_memory` through a Hosted profile therefore inherits them unchanged — which is the substance of Decision 2's rejected alternative as well.

One qualification worth stating plainly: `relation_review.py` guards *supersession integrity*, not *judgement*. It cannot stop an agent from superseding a good conclusion with a worse one. The teaching that mitigates that is the skill, which is why v3 ships one.

### Decision 4: a candidate registry replaces pairwise `== LIFECYCLE_CANDIDATE` branching

`hosted_plugins.py` gains `CANDIDATE_PROFILES` (candidate -> profile) and `RECORDS_CANDIDATES` (candidates that expose `record_memory` and therefore pin `minimum_records_reader_version: 2` and bind selection cases). Every `candidate == LIFECYCLE_CANDIDATE` site that meant "this candidate carries the records lifecycle" becomes `candidate in RECORDS_CANDIDATES`, and `_selection_cases` / `_validate_records_acceptance` take the candidate instead of assuming v2 — so v3 is held to *at least* v2's acceptance bar rather than silently falling through to the weaker default-candidate path.

v3 carries its own byte-identical copies of `selection-cases.json` and the `exomem-records` skill rather than reaching into v2's directory. Candidate packages must be self-contained and immutable: v2's `skills_sha256` and `selection_cases_sha256` are pinned in its committed lock, and a shared file would let a v3 edit silently invalidate a v2 release identity.

**Those selection cases do not cover what v3 adds, and that is a promotion gate.** `selection-cases.json` is a *Records* selection matrix: `_selection_cases` hard-requires exactly four cases over one axis — append versus proposal, per client — and `_validate_records_acceptance` binds that exact matrix into the live acceptance verifier. The schema cannot express the discrimination v3 actually introduces, which is the confusable write trio: when should an agent `remember` a new page, `edit_memory` an existing one, and `replace_memory` a superseded one? Nothing in this change produces evidence that a model routes that trio correctly. Extending the matrix would mean changing the shape of a released acceptance contract and its verifier, which is out of scope for an unpromotable candidate. **v3 must not be promoted until a write-verb selection matrix exists and passes**, and that is recorded here rather than left to be discovered at promotion time.

### Decision 5: the supersession teaching lives in a v3-only skill

The shared skills under `plugins/hosted/skills/` are rendered into every candidate, and `tests/test_hosted_plugin_skills.py` asserts that none of them mention `edit_memory` or `replace_memory`. That assertion is correct: those skills also ship inside v1 and v2, where the tools do not exist, and `validate_skill_text` fails closed when a skill names a tool the profile lacks.

So the new teaching lands as `plugins/hosted/candidates/hosted-alpha-agent-v3/skills/exomem-supersede/SKILL.md`, alongside the v3 copy of `exomem-records`. It teaches when to supersede versus correct in place, that a supersession is a two-step validate-then-commit flow, and that a durable intent is stated with `plan_memory` before the work rather than reconstructed after it.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- **Risk: a wider Hosted profile invites low-quality revision.** Mitigated by the leaf-level guardrails in Decision 3 and by shipping teaching with the tools rather than tools alone. Accepted: accumulation-only is the worse failure, and it is the one the product thesis names.
- **Risk: profile membership accidentally moves the local tool surface.** `tests/fixtures/mcp_tool_schemas.json` and `src/exomem/tool_surface_contract.json` are generated from the full local server, not from any profile, so they must not move. This change asserts that and treats any movement as release-blocking, because a changed ChatGPT Personal Plugin fingerprint forces a re-registration. (The fingerprint is already `pending` with `refresh_required: true` for an unrelated earlier surface change; this change must not add to it.)
- **Risk: the candidate-registry refactor perturbs v2.** Mitigated by asserting that v2's committed `compatibility.json`, lock, and archive bytes still match a fresh render, and that the v1 release-identity fixture still verifies.
- **Trade-off: v3 is Claude-only at launch.** ChatGPT users keep the accumulation-only surface until the OpenAI channel gets its own acceptance run. Accepted deliberately per Decision 2; the alternative is shipping an unrefreshed action schema into the exact failure mode already on record.
- **Trade-off: `.github/workflows/` is not extended to render or check v3.** That directory is out of scope for this change, so v3 freshness is enforced by a repository test that calls `hosted_plugins.check(candidate="hosted-alpha-agent-v3", platform="claude")` rather than by a CI render step. A follow-up should add the CI step for symmetry with v2.
